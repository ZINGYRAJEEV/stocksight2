# Stock Analysis Workflow
*A step-by-step guide for analyzing a stock manually, organized by company type. Based on notes from the [Basic to Advance Complete Investment Course](https://www.financiallyfree.in/watch-player/basic-to-advance-complete-investment-course).*

**Key websites/tools referenced throughout:**
| Site/Tool | Use it for |
|---|---|
| [screener.in](https://www.screener.in) | Financials, ratios, PE history, shareholding pattern, credit ratings, concalls, annual reports, custom stock screens |
| [Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com) | Broker research reports, YouTube channel for concalls |
| [Tijori Finance](https://www.tijorifinance.com) | Investor press releases, company data |
| [Value Pickr](https://forum.valuepickr.com) | Open-source investor discussion forum |
| [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com) | Company culture / employee ratings (look for **3.0+**) |
| Google / YouTube | Promoter background checks, scam history, management interviews |
| Any online CAGR calculator | Quick sales/profit/price CAGR |

## STEP 0 — Categorize the Company (do this first, always)

Before doing anything else, work out which bucket the stock falls into — the rest of your workflow depends on it.

**Where:** screener.in → Peer comparison + Profit & Loss section (last 5–10 years of sales & profit growth).

| If sales growth + profit growth is... | Category |
|---|---|
| ≥15% and ≥15% | **Fast Grower** |
| ~10–15% | **Stalwart** (medium grower) |
| At/below India's GDP growth rate | **Slow Grower** |
| Swings with demand/supply, no pricing power | **Cyclical** |
| Currently distressed but showing recovery signs | **Turnaround** |

Jump to the matching workflow below.

---

## WORKFLOW A — Fast Growers

*Target: ≥15% sales growth AND ≥15% profit growth, consistently.*

1. **Confirm the growth numbers.**
   - Where: screener.in → Profit & Loss → Compounded Sales Growth & Compounded Profit Growth.
   - Cross-check with a CAGR calculator using raw sales/profit figures if you want to verify a specific period.
2. **Identify what's driving the growth.**
   - Look for: reinvestment into the business, innovation, new technology, branding (especially B2C), efficiency gains.
   - Where: Annual report (company website or screener.in → Documents), investor presentations (screener.in → Documents → Presentations).
3. **Find out where future growth will come from.** Check management's own guidance for:
   - Industry tailwind, new product launches, market share gains, geographic expansion, efficiency-driven earnings growth.
   - Where: Quarterly concalls (screener.in → Concalls tab, or Trendlyne's YouTube channel), investor presentations.
4. **Check valuation.**
   - Where: screener.in → PE chart (compare current PE to historical median, and to industry PE).
   - Low PE + rising earnings = good sign. If unsure how to weigh growth into the comparison, use PEG (see Step 5 of Workflow F).
5. **Run management & quality checks** — see the shared "Story Building" section below before deciding.

---

## WORKFLOW B — Stalwarts (Medium Growers)

*Target: ~10–15% annual growth, steady and stable — think Hindustan Lever, Dabur.*

1. **Confirm steady, stable growth** (not spiky) over several years.
   - Where: screener.in → Profit & Loss, look for consistency, not just the average.
2. **Check margin stability.** Margins should be stable year to year — a wobble here is a caution flag.
   - Where: screener.in → Profit & Loss → Operating Profit Margin (OPM) row.
3. **Check valuation against its own history.**
   - Where: screener.in → PE chart. Buy signal = trading **at or below historical mean PE**.
4. **Confirm it's still "good, stable, with future plans."**
   - Where: annual report + investor presentation for forward guidance.
5. **Decide entry/exit using the Peter Lynch rule of thumb:** buy for a 30–50% gain, and consider trimming once:
   - It's already delivered that 30–40% return, AND/OR
   - It's trading at a high multiple relative to its own average PE, AND/OR
   - Company signals turn negative (concalls, news).
6. **Position sizing:** these are your "ballast" — consider keeping around 30% of the portfolio here for stability.

## WORKFLOW C — Cyclicals

*Target: businesses tied to demand/supply cycles (cotton, cement, paper, other commodities) — no pricing power, high volatility.*

1. **Confirm demand/supply balance.** You want to catch it when supply and demand are roughly in balance and margins are trending up — not already at a cyclical peak.
   - Where: industry news, company concalls, broker reports (Trendlyne).
2. **Track the margin trail as your timing signal.**
   - Where: screener.in → Profit & Loss → Gross margin → Company margin → **Operating Profit Margin (OPM)**. Rising OPM with room to keep rising = good entry window.
3. **Watch for early insider-buying signals** — often shows up as unusual price strength before the broader market catches on.
   - Where: screener.in → Shareholding Pattern (promoter buying), price chart.
4. **Check seasonal patterns** — my notes flag checking price behavior around March specifically; verify this against the course material for the specific sector you're looking at.
5. **Cross-check with a real case study before committing** (e.g., building materials: cement, cables, MDF wood) — read investor presentations for new capacity additions and watch YouTube interviews of board members.

---

## WORKFLOW D — Turnarounds (distressed / recovering)

*Examples from notes: Apple (historical), Big Bazaar, Yes Bank, CG Power, Ruchi Soya.*

1. **Look for the trigger events that suggest a real turnaround is underway:**
   - Change in management, change in strategy, new product launches, favorable government policy shifts.
   - Where: news, annual report, management commentary in concalls.
2. **Check the shareholding pattern** for signs of confidence (institutional or promoter buying).
   - Where: screener.in → Shareholding Pattern.
3. **Verify debt is actually being reduced**, not just talked about.
   - Where: screener.in → Balance Sheet → Borrowings (year-on-year trend).
4. **Confirm new launches/improvements are real**, not just announced.
   - Where: investor presentations, concalls, news coverage.
5. **Sanity-check management credibility** before trusting the turnaround story — see "Management Checks" below. This category has the highest risk of a broken promise, so weight this step heavily.

---

## WORKFLOW E — Slow Growers

*Growth at or below India's GDP growth rate. Generally: a fixed deposit may be a better use of money.*

1. **Confirm it really is a slow grower** and not a temporarily depressed fast grower or stalwart.
   - Where: screener.in → Profit & Loss, several years of history.
2. **Diagnose why it's slow** — this tells you whether it's a "hold and collect dividends" story or a "avoid" story:
   - Complacent management, "large fish in small pond," rising competition, or genuine business disruption/obsolescence.
   - Where: annual report, management discussion & analysis (MD&A) section, industry news.
3. **Default position:** unless there's a specific reason to hold (e.g., strong dividend yield, deep undervaluation), this category is usually a pass in favor of safer instruments.

## SHARED STEP — "Building the Story" (do this for every serious candidate, any category)

This is the qualitative research layer that sits under every workflow above. Order: **Management → Industry → Risk.**

1. **Management check.**
   - Google the promoter/company name + "scam" or "fraud."
   - Watch YouTube interviews of the promoter/board for tone and growth mindset.
   - Compare promoter salary to industry peers — pay tied to profit % is a good sign.
   - Where: screener.in → Annual Report → Remuneration section.
   - **Rule: if you don't trust the promoter, don't invest — no exceptions.**
2. **Company culture check.**
   - Where: Glassdoor.com — look for a rating of **3.0 or higher**.
3. **Business understanding.**
   - Is it a sticky/repeat-purchase business? Is it cyclical? What's driving profit?
   - Where: investor presentation (company site or screener.in), annual report.
4. **Industry positioning.**
   - Overall market share, overall industry growth, headwind vs. tailwind.
   - Where: broker reports (Trendlyne), investor presentations, Value Pickr forum discussions.
5. **Risk identification.**
   - Where: screener.in → Credit Rating section, annual report risk factors, Value Pickr for community-flagged risks.
6. **Bank-specific check (if applicable):** NPA (Non-Performing Assets) and ROA (Return on Assets).
   - Where: screener.in financials for the bank.
7. **Write the one-paragraph story.** Use this structure (modeled on the LT Foods example from my notes): *what's the core business, is it fast/slow growing, what new segment(s) are growing faster, and what is management guiding toward (e.g., margin expansion)?* A clear story is what lets you make buy/sell/hold calls without getting rattled by news noise.

## SHARED STEP — Valuation Toolkit (apply after the story checks out)

Run these where relevant — not every method suits every category:

1. **PE Ratio** — compare current PE to industry PE and to the stock's own historical median PE (screener.in PE chart). Low relative to history = potential buy.
2. **PEG Ratio** — use when comparing companies with different growth rates: `PEG = PE / Growth rate`.
   - PEG < 1 → cheap/buy · PEG = 1 → fair value/hold · **PEG > 1 → expensive/avoid** *(note: my original notes had a typo listing "<1" for both the cheap and expensive cases — double check this against the course video)*.
3. **DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)** — `Present Value = Amount / (1 + interest)^Years`. If current price is 30–40% below your calculated intrinsic value, that's a reasonable buy zone. Use a risk-free bond/treasury rate as your benchmark.
4. **Discounted PE Method** — project earnings forward at an assumed growth rate, apply a PE multiple to get a future price, then discount that back to today at your own required rate of return. **My rule: if the price falls to/below this discounted fair value, that's my buy signal.**
5. Remember: none of PE, PEG, or DCF capture company *quality* on their own — that's what the "Story" step above is for.

## SHARED STEP — Building a Segment-wise Revenue & Blended Growth Model

Once you've written the "story" (Step 7 above) — e.g., a slow-growing core business plus one or more faster-growing new segments — build this out as a simple spreadsheet model so you can see the *blended* growth rate the segments produce together. This is the LT Foods-style model referenced earlier (Basmati = slow grower, Organic + New Products = fast growers).

**1. Lay out one row pair per segment, going forward in yearly columns.**
For each segment, use two rows: a **Revenue** row and a **Growth %** row directly beneath it.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segment A Revenue | (actual) | (projected) | (projected) | |
| Segment A Growth | | =formula | =formula | |
| Segment B Revenue | (actual) | (projected) | (projected) | |
| Segment B Growth | | =formula | =formula | |
| ... | | | | |
| **Total** | =SUM of segments | =SUM of segments | =SUM of segments | |
| **Blended Growth** | | =formula | =formula | |

**2. Growth formula (the core formula to reuse everywhere):**
```
= ((This Year - Last Year) / Last Year) * 100
```
In actual cell references, e.g. comparing column J to column I on row 248:
```
=((J248-I248)/I248)*100
```
Apply this same formula under **every** segment's revenue row, and again on the **Total** row to get your blended/overall growth rate. Drag it across the remaining year columns once it's right for one column.

**3. Project each segment's revenue forward** using its own growth assumption:
```
= Prior Year Revenue * (1 + Assumed Growth Rate)
```
e.g. `=I243*(1+J244)` — this is how the "New Products" row was projected in the LT Foods model. Set a distinct, explicit growth assumption per segment per year (they don't have to stay constant — e.g., New Products growth can taper from 40% → 35% → 30% → 25% as the base gets bigger).

**4. Sum the segments into a Total row**, and apply the same growth formula (Step 2) to the Total row to get the **blended growth rate** — this shows you the company-wide growth even though no single segment is growing at that exact rate.

**5. Read the model.** The value of this exercise: a slow-core-business-with-a-fast-growing-new-segment story is only as good as the blended number. If Basmati (core, ~9%) is 80%+ of revenue, the blended growth will stay close to 9–11% even with Organic/New Products growing 20–40% — so check whether the fast segment is actually big enough yet to move the needle, or whether it's a multi-year story still building scale.

**6. Where to source the growth assumptions:** don't guess — pull historical segment growth rates from the annual report's segment reporting note, and forward assumptions from management guidance in investor presentations or concalls (screener.in → Concalls / Documents).

## SHARED STEP — Before You Pull the Trigger

1. **Portfolio rules:**
   - Avoid companies below ₹100–500 cr market cap, or cap any single position at 5% of the portfolio.
   - Target **15–20 stocks total** — enough to diversify, few enough to stay concentrated on conviction ideas.
   - "Water the flowers, not the weeds" — add to positions that are working, don't keep propping up losers.
2. **Bias check** — pause and ask if you're falling for:
   - Confirmation bias, recency bias, anchoring bias, or herd mentality.
3. Re-read your one-paragraph "story" one more time — does the price still make sense against it?

## Quick-Fire Numbers Checklist (final pass, any category)

- Sales growth trend & compounded sales growth
- Profit growth trend & compounded profit growth
- Margin trend (should be stable or improving)
- Operating profit margin trend
- Interest cost — should be reducing year on year
- Tax paid — falling steadily can be a caution flag, not just a positive
- Net profit growth rate
- Google sanity check for fraud/scandal
- Geographic expansion or new product launches in the pipeline
